Friends in Port Phillip Bay

Meet the animals that live in Port Phillip Bay, right in your own backyard!

Meet the animals

Tap a button to find out more about each one.

Weedy Seadragon

The bay’s flowy friend.       

  • Cousin of the seahorse. Its leafy bits sway like seaweed, so it hides in plain sight.
  • Dad does the babysitting! The male carries the eggs on his tail until they hatch.
  • Look for me: around jetties and seaweed beds. I’m shy, so look very closely.

Little Penguin

St Kilda and Phillip Islands finest feathery friends.

  • Tiny but tough. Little penguins are about as tall as a ruler and can hide in between rocks.
  • Home time is dusk. They swim and fish all day, then waddle back to the rocks at sunset.
  • Look for me: At the St Kilda breakwater. Stay quiet as a mouse, don't use your torch my eyes are sensitive!
 

Burrunan Dolphin

Port Phillips very own dolphin .

  • A newly named species. Scientists only gave me my name in 2011.
  • Home all year. A few of us live in the bay instead of travelling far away each year.
  • Look for me: on boat tours or from the shore on a calm day. Binoculars help!
 

Giant Spider Crab

A hard shell but soft inside.

  • Shell swap. Us crabs outgrow our shells, so each winter we shed the old one and grow a new one.
  • Safety in numbers. Me and my friends gather by the thousands and pile up while our new shells are soft.
  • Look for me: under piers on the bay in winter. Look, but don’t touch!
  

Australasian gannet

A seabird that dives like a dart.

  • Plunge! I fold my wings and dive into the water to catch fish.
  • A big nesting spot. Gannets nest on Pope’s Eye, near the mouth of the bay.
  • Look for me: over the water, high up, before I dive down and make a splash!
 

Blue-ringed Octopus

A small, shy, eight legged friend, who happens to be very venomous.

  • Rings that flash. I get bright blue rings when I feel scared. That means im asking you to “keep away!”
  • Never touch. I might be tiny, but my bite is dangerous. Make sure to tell a grown up if you spot me.
  • Look for me: in rock pools. I love to squeeze in to small spaces, but no touching!.

Be a Bay Hero

Rubbish on the beach, or in the street, can end up in the bay. But you can help!

1

Take it home or bin it. Please pack up every wrapper, bottle and bag before you leave to help your new friends in the sea.

2

Streets lead to the sea. Drains carry litter to the bay, so bin it wherever you are.

3

Pick up three. Try to find 3 pieces of rubbish wherever you are and throw them away, make sure you are being safe and washing your hands afterwards.

4

Give animals space. Watch quietly from far away and never chase them, that way they might come back to say 'Hi' again.
